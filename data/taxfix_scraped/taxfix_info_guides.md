## Living Separately or Divorced?

Only those who are **married and living together** can submit a joint tax return. One day in the entire year is enough. # Small business regulation (Kleinunternehmerregelung)

## Important information

* This question is about applying the small business regulation (Kleinunternehmerregelung § 19 UStG) in 2024.
* If you use this rule, you must not show VAT on your invoices and cannot deduct input tax from the tax office.

## Understanding the question

This question is important to determine whether your tax return will treat you as a small business (Kleinunternehmer) or as VAT-liable.

As “Kleinunternehmer”, you didn’t have to pay VAT to the tax office – but you also couldn’t claim any input tax back.

## Your answer

Choose the right option:

* **Yes:** You applied the small business regulation for all of 2024, so you didn’t charge VAT or claim input tax. Example: You issued invoices without VAT and didn’t deduct any input tax.
* **No:** You charged VAT or claimed input tax from the tax office. Example: You showed VAT on your invoices or submitted VAT returns.

## Income Categories

### Pension
Select this item if a pension was received in the tax year. 

* **Types included:** The type of pension is irrelevant, e.g. old-age pension, widow's pension, disability pension, orphan's pension, etc. 
* **Tax statement details:** Pension payments (these are shown in the income tax statement under number 8) also fall under this point.

### Letting and leasing
This item includes income from renting and/or leasing your own property, e.g. a condominium or holiday home. 

* **Included property & funds:** Undeveloped land is also included here as well as income from a closed real estate fund.

### Photovoltaic / Solar
This includes income from operating your own photovoltaic systems. In this case, electricity produced by solar energy is fed into the public grid and paid for by the grid operator.

* **Eligible systems:** The type of system is irrelevant, which is why roof systems including solar roof tiles, façade systems and balcony power plants etc. are all included. 
* **Commercial income criteria:** The amount of income is also irrelevant as long as there is an intention to make a profit (the income should be higher than the expenditure in the long term). This then constitutes commercial income. 
* **Tax-free limits/conditions:** However, under certain conditions (for single-family homes including outbuildings such as garages, garden sheds or carports, the system may have a gross output of up to **30KW**; for other buildings, an output of up to **15KW** per residential/commercial unit), these are tax-free.

### Agriculture and forestry
This includes income from the operation of agriculture, forestry, viticulture, horticulture or tree nurseries as well as income from inland shipping, fish farming and beekeeping or income from a secondary agricultural and forestry business.

### From deferral models or family foundations
* **Deferral models:** Income from deferral models is income from financial models in which payments or taxes are postponed for as long as possible in order to achieve short-term benefits. 
* **Family foundations:** Income from family foundations is money or assets that a foundation passes on to family members, for example in the form of regular payments or lump sums.

# Allocate VAT

## Important Information

* The standard rate of VAT is 19% and applies to most goods and services. The reduced rate of 7% applies, for example, to food, books and cultural services. Some income is exempt from VAT (0%), e.g. for small businesses or certain professional groups.

## Understanding the Question

You have stated that you apply mixed VAT rates to your income. Please now enter how your income is broken down across the different tax rates. ## Your Answer

# Room costs

Costs for a separate study can only be declared if a specially furnished room is used that is used (almost) exclusively for work. In addition, the study must be the centre of all business and professional activities.
If this is not the case, the daily allowance for working from home can still be used.

## Separate study
There must be a room used specifically for work (with four walls and a door). This means that a work corner in the living room or similar is out of the question. More than 90% of the room must be used for work. It should not be used for private purposes, e.g. as a guest room. The tax office often requires proof of this in the form of a floor plan and even photos of the room.

## Centre of the entire activity
All or the majority of the activities relevant to the work must be carried out in the study. They must not only be secondary tasks. If this is the case, the study is the centre of activity if you work more than 50% from home in the study.

## Good to know
The lump sum and the room costs cannot be declared at the same time for the same period.

By the way: Costs for a desk, a lamp or other items for working do not count as costs for the study. These are so-called work equipment without these requirements. We will ask you about these items elsewhere and help you to deduct all relevant costs.

## Key Info

Assess whether your use of the room is nearly all work-related. More than minimal private use will typically disqualify the room.

- For roles typically performed elsewhere (e.g., teaching at school), the center is usually not the home office.

## Understanding the question
Decide whether the core, defining work outputs happen in the home office. If so, it may be the center of your overall activity.

# Number of months

The requirements are met if a separate study is used (almost) exclusively for work and this also represents the centre of the entire business and professional activity.

## Separate study
It must be a separate room with four walls and a door for the activity. The room must be used almost exclusively for professional purposes, i.e. more than 90%. This means that a work corner in the living room, the kitchen table or the guest room do not count as a study.

## Centre of activity
This is the case if all or the majority of the activities relevant to the work can be carried out in the study, not just ancillary tasks. If this is the case, the study is the centre of activity if you work more than 50% from home in the study.

Enter the number of months you worked from home here. It does not matter how many days per month or week you worked from home, as long as the above requirements are met (separate room and centre of activity).

# Recording work equipment - this is how it works

## Important information
- Work equipment includes, for example, computer hardware, software costs, office supplies, specialised literature, magazines and other operating resources.
- As soon as the net purchase price **does not exceed €800**, you can deduct the work equipment **immediately as a business expense**.
- If the net price exceeds the €800 limit or if the work equipment is used for more than one year, you must depreciate it **over its useful life** (i.e. not completely in the year of purchase).

## Understanding the question
The question asks which of the above-mentioned operating resources you purchased in the year in question and would like to claim for tax purposes. This information influences your profit calculation or your business expense declaration and is important so that the tax office can understand whether the correct depreciation rules are being applied.

## Your answer
Choose one (or more) of the following options, depending on which work equipment you have declared in the year:
- **Computer hardware**: e.g. laptop, desktop PC, monitor or peripherals that you use for your professional activity.
- **Software costs**: e.g. purchase of programs, licences or apps that you use professionally.
- **Office supplies (e.g. pen, paper)**: Small office or stationery items such as paper, printer cartridges, writing pads.
- **Technical literature**: Books or printed technical journals that you use professionally.
- **Journals**: Regularly published trade magazines or journals that you subscribe to and use professionally.
- **Other work equipment**: All other work equipment that does not fall under the other categories - e.g. tools, measuring devices or accessory hardware.

Note: For each item of work equipment specified, check whether the costs were **below €800 net**. If so, you can deduct it in full in the year of purchase. If the amount is higher or if you use the work equipment for more than one year, you must depreciate it over its useful life (entry in the "Depreciation" section).

# Gifts to business partners or customers

## Important information
- Gifts to business partners or customers are only tax-deductible if they cost **no more than €50 gross per recipient per year** (if you are not entitled to deduct input tax).
- In addition, the gifts must have a **business reason** - i.e. they must be business-related, without consideration, and must not be for private reasons.
- For each gift, you must document exactly: **recipient (name), occasion, value and date** - and prove whether the gift was taxed by the recipient or whether you paid tax on it at a flat rate in accordance with Section 37b EStG.

## Understanding the question
This question asks whether you gave gifts to business partners, customers or other business contacts in the year in question - and whether you have documented all the necessary information. This is because only then are these expenses tax-deductible under the conditions of tax law. If the conditions are not met (e.g. value over €50 or incomplete documentation), a tax deduction is not possible.

## Your answer
Select "Yes" if **all** of the following points apply:
- You gave gifts that were business-related (e.g. customer gift, thank you, business relationship).
- The value of all gifts per recipient per year does **not exceed €50** (net or gross, depending on the input tax deduction).
- You have **documented who** you have given what to: Name of the recipient, occasion, date and value.
- You can prove whether the gift was taxed by the recipient or whether you paid tax on it as a lump sum in accordance with § 37b EStG.

Select "No" if **one or more** of the points listed do not apply - e.g. value over €50, gifts without a business reason, lack of documentation or unclear taxation.

# Waste disposal (VAT determination)

## Important information
- The method of waste disposal determines whether you can claim back the VAT you have paid as input tax.
- Local authority charges for refuse collection do not usually include VAT.
- Invoices from private waste disposal companies often show VAT.

## Understanding the question
This question is important for determining whether VAT was payable on your waste disposal costs. If, as a business owner, you are entitled to deduct input VAT, you can claim a refund of the VAT paid (input tax) from the tax office. Municipal services are usually billed via public charges that do not include VAT. Private waste disposal companies, on the other hand, generally issue invoices showing VAT.

## Your answer
Please select all options that apply to your waste disposal:
- **Local authority refuse collection**: Select this option if your waste disposal is handled by the local council or town council and you receive a fee notice for this. These notices do not usually include VAT, so no input tax deduction is possible in this case.
- **Private waste disposal company**: Select this option if you have a contract with a private company for waste disposal. Invoices from these companies usually show VAT as a separate line item, which you can claim as input tax under certain conditions.

# Movable assets, maintenance expenses, loan interest and other costs

## Key information
- **Rental costs for movable assets** include, for example, lease payments for work equipment such as computers, copiers or machines that you didn't buy but rented or leased.
- **Maintenance expenses** are costs for repairs or upkeep – for instance, if you renovate your office or get work-related equipment repaired.
- **Loan interest** applies if you took out a loan for your self-employment, e.g. to finance your setup – the interest is tax-deductible.
- **Other expenses** covers additional business costs that don't fit any other category, such as membership fees, training costs or bank charges.

## Understanding the question
This question is about identifying any additional expenses related to your self-employment. These costs can lower your tax burden because they're considered business expenses. The categories mentioned are typical for self-employed people – it's all about capturing everything you paid for in connection with your work.

## Your answer (Multi Select)
Select all types of expenses you had in 2025 related to your self-employment:
- **Rental of movable assets**: Choose this if you rented or leased any work-related items. *Examples*: Leasing a laptop, monthly rental for a POS system, tool or machine rental fees.
- **Maintenance expenses**: Pick this if you had to repair or maintain something. *Examples*: Electrical repairs in your home office, servicing a work bike.
- **Loan interest**: If you took out a loan – e.g. for office equipment or to bridge cash gaps – you can claim the interest here. *Note*: Only the interest is deductible – not the repayments!
- **Other expenses**: For anything that doesn't clearly fit elsewhere but is still business-related. *Examples*: Costs for professional coaching, association membership fees, industry literature or business account fees.

# File tax return

## Trade income
Trade income is the calculated profit plus additions from Section 8 of the Trade Tax Act (GewStG) and minus deductions from Section 9 GewStG. These are either large amounts or special cases that in most cases do not apply to small businesses. However, always check whether any of these apply.

## Mandatory Filing
Based on Section 25 of the Trade Tax Implementation Ordinance (GewStDV), a trade tax return must be submitted in the following cases:
- if the trade income is more than €24,500 in the tax year
- for corporations such as AGs, GmbHs etc., if they are not exempt from trade tax
- for co-operatives and mutual insurance companies if they are not exempt from trade tax
- other legal entities under private law and associations without legal personality if their business operations (excluding agriculture and forestry) generate a trade income of more than €5,000 in the tax year
- for companies of legal entities under public law, if they are standing commercial enterprises and their trade income exceeds €5,000 in the tax year
- for institutions exempt from trade tax pursuant to Section 3 Nos. 5, 6, 8, 9, 15, 17, 21, 26, 27, 28 and 29 GewStG if they carry out an activity subject to trade tax and their trade income exceeds €5,000 in the tax year
- for companies for which losses that can be carried forward were determined separately in the previous year
- if the tax office specifically requires a trade tax return
